Development of a Master Console for Robotic-Guided Catheterization

UCL BEng Final Year Project

Designed a 2-DOF control handle that replicates the natural push-pull and rotation of catheterization, replacing the four-joystick console on an existing MR-conditional heart catheterisation robot.

  • Designed and built the 2-DOF handle: CNC-machined shaft, 3D-printed handle and mounts, and a belt drive that re-centres the handle for unlimited push-pull travel.
  • Integrated dual magnetic encoders to map handle motion into control signals (0.022° rotation resolution).
  • Added an Arduino capacitive grip sensor so commands are only sent while the clinician is holding the handle.
  • Evaluated path-tracking accuracy: root-mean-square error of <2.0 mm across preset reference profiles.

The console has been validated on the bench. The next step is integrating it with the robot for further functional testing, followed by phantom testing.

Design and Optimization of a Pump Impeller for Ventricular Assist Devices

Designed a pump impeller for a ventricular assist device, targeting a 0.8 bar pressure head and 75 L/min flow rate while keeping shear stress within safe limits for blood.

  • Optimized the blade geometry — inner and outer radius, height, angle, number of blades and diffuser area — in a design spreadsheet.
  • Set the operating speed and preset physical and estimated parameters to calculate blockage factors, flow rate and pressure rise.
  • Ran CFD at 2,500 RPM to check internal flow patterns and critical shear stresses.
  • Prototyped with 3D printing and tested on an impeller test rig.
